# Origins of the *Star Fleet Technical Manual* (Franz Joseph, Ballantine, 1975)

Research notes for a tribute project. Compiled 7 November 2026.

## How to read this report

Each claim carries a status tag:

- **CONFIRMED**: two or more sources that are at least partly independent agree.
- **SINGLE-SOURCE**: one source only, or several sources that all trace back to one origin.
- **DISPUTED**: the sources disagree. The disagreement is described.

**A caveat on independence.** Most of what is known about the book's origins comes from three primary sources:

1. Franz Joseph's own interviews (1976 and 1982).
2. His daughter Karen Dick's 1999 interview.
3. A timeline Karen compiled from his "Writing Activities Log."

All three are hosted on Greg Tyler's fan site, trekplace.com. Wikipedia, Memory Alpha and Bob Rehak's academic work mostly cite these same documents. So when an encyclopedia "agrees" with Karen's timeline, that is often one source repeated, not two independent ones. Where this happens, the claim is marked SINGLE-SOURCE (family/primary) or the shared origin is noted.

Quotations are kept under 15 words. Everything else is paraphrased.

### The Blueprints came first, but the dates need correcting

The prompt calls the *Star Trek Blueprints* a 1973 work. That is only partly right.

| Claim | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| He drew the Blueprints (the *Booklet of General Ship's Plans*) in the middle of the Manual project, as a side project. He needed a full ship layout before he could draw interior details for the Manual. | **CONFIRMED** (FJ 1976 and the timeline) | [S4], [S2] |
| He began the Blueprints on 21 September 1973, finished them on 14 December 1973 and sent a check print to Roddenberry. Roddenberry phoned on New Year's Eve to praise them. | **CONFIRMED** (FJ 1982 and the timeline) | [S5], [S2] |
| First "edition": 500 hand-cut, hand-stapled diazo blueprint booklets, assembled by S.T.A.R. members in his house and sold under a one-off Paramount licence at **Equicon '74 (April 1974)**. 410 sold, and 450 more were requested. | **CONFIRMED** (timeline; Karen's recollection; Rehak) | [S2], [S3 p.4], [S15] |
| One uncut copy went to the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum for its "Life in the Universe" display, at Roddenberry's suggestion (1974). | **CONFIRMED** (timeline; Newitt intro) | [S2], [S5] |
| Ballantine's mass-market Blueprints came out in **1975**. | **DISPUTED** on month | Timeline: in stores 24 May 1975 [S2]. Memory Alpha: April 1975 [S13]. Rehak: on sale May 1975 [S15]. |

**Bottom line:** the Blueprints were drawn in 1973, privately printed in 1974, and commercially published in 1975, about six months before the Manual.

### Why he did it

His motives, paraphrased from his own interviews:

- **Accuracy.** He wanted the work to be "technically and scientifically correct," an extension of real engineering [S5].
- **Fairness to fans.** Karen was paying six dollars a time for poor mimeographed scripts, and he felt the fans were being short-changed. He wanted to give them "the real thing" [S5].
- **Education.** He hoped to steer young fans toward real engineering and science [S4], [S5].
- **Not a fan.** He said repeatedly that he was not a *Star Trek* fan. His interest was in a plausible future spacefaring society and space force [S4], [S5]. Karen confirms that he objected to the show's scientific errors [S3 p.3].
- **Care with Roddenberry's universe.** He was wary of building on another man's creation and did not want to dictate to Roddenberry [S4], [S5], [S3 p.5].

Short quotes:

- "technically and scientifically correct" (FJ, 1982 [S5])
- "I've never been a fan of the show" (FJ, 1976 [S4])

### The book as an "in-universe" document

**SINGLE-SOURCE / CONFIRMED** by the book's structure as described in [S13], [S11], [S6] and [S3].

- **The premise.** The Manual presents itself as data accidentally downloaded to 20th-century Earth during "Tomorrow Is Yesterday" (the Omaha Air Force base episode).
- **The forewords.** Foreword I is a 1973 memo from a fictional US Director of Security Control. Foreword II is a Star Fleet memo dated stardate 3150.10 [S13].
- **The cut-off.** FJ tried to treat stardate 3113 ("Tomorrow Is Yesterday") as the cut-off point for content [S3 p.5].
- **Format.** Material is organised as numbered Technical Orders (T.O.s) under a master outline. FJ said the outline could classify tens of thousands of subjects: 150,000 in 1982 [S5], 50,000 in 1976 [S4]. That is a small inconsistency.
- **Staying in character.** He answered fan mail in character as a Federation official on "Star Fleet" stationery, and appeared at conventions in character, with his "ship" the courier *Cygnus* in orbit [S6]. Some readers took it literally. A JPL/Caltech coordinator tried to order the fictional security docket by mail [S4], [S6].
- **Credits.** He kept credit lines minimal because a normal credit page would break the illusion [S6].

### The falling-out with Roddenberry and the canon question

- **Early friction (CONFIRMED in the family sources).** On 20 August 1975 Roddenberry wrote to say the Manual's acknowledgement of him was too small [S2]. FJ said he only ever heard this complaint through the fan grapevine [S6], which slightly contradicts the timeline entry.
- **Karen's four reasons for Roddenberry's resentment (SINGLE-SOURCE).**
  1. The lost Lincoln Enterprises deal. FJ went over Roddenberry's head to Paramount.
  2. The *Planet Earth* rights dispute.
  3. The credit-line complaint.
  4. Once the film was under way, neither Roddenberry nor Paramount owned FJ's original designs, because the copyright was in FJ's name [S3 p.2, p.5].
- **The "military" question.** Roddenberry later said Starfleet was not military. FJ countered that "Star Fleet Armed Forces" was on the earliest T.O.s Roddenberry saw and he never objected [S6], [S3 p.1].
- **"Fan kook" (SINGLE-SOURCE, Karen).** In the mid-1980s, on CompuServe, Michael Okuda called FJ a "fan kook" whose work Roddenberry never approved. In 1999 Okuda contacted Karen and explained that the remark came from a patronising in-character reply FJ had sent him in the 1970s. He planned a positive sidebar on FJ in a book that was then cancelled [S3 p.6].
- **"Roddenberry's Rules of Starship Design."** These rules (paired nacelles, both visible from the front, and so on) rule out the Dreadnought, Destroyer, Scout and Tug. They are said to have come from Roddenberry via Probert. Karen argues they were written to invalidate FJ's ships [S3 p.5]. Tyler also notes the rules, calling them "supposedly" issued [S7]. **SINGLE-SOURCE** on the motive.
- **Canon status (CONFIRMED).** The Manual is not treated as canon. Official encyclopedias ignore it, and canon is generally limited to what appears on screen [S8], [S7], [S13].
- **TNG "All Good Things…" (1994).** Its three-nacelle future *Enterprise* is often read as a nod to the Dreadnought. Karen says designer Greg Jein believed the three-nacelle idea was his own [S3 p.4]. **SINGLE-SOURCE**.
- **Karen's verdict.** She concluded in 1999 that it was a slow-motion series of misunderstandings rather than villainy on anyone's side [S3 p.6].

### Influence on later technical manuals and fan design

- **A new genre (CONFIRMED).** The Blueprints and Manual started the "technical manual / blueprint" genre for fictional spacecraft [S10], [S14], [S15]. FJ himself claimed that about 150 imitations followed [S6] (**SINGLE-SOURCE**).
- **Fan successors (CONFIRMED).** These include the *Star Fleet Medical Reference Manual* (1977), Klingon and Romulan blueprints, and Geoffrey Mandel's *Starfleet Handbook* [S5], [S15].
- **Official successors (CONFIRMED as lineage; influence is the commentators' interpretation).** *Mr. Scott's Guide to the Enterprise* (Shane Johnson, 1987), the *TNG Technical Manual* (Sternbach and Okuda, 1991) and the *DS9 Technical Manual* (1998) followed [S14], [S15].
- **Games (CONFIRMED).** Lou Zocchi's *Star Fleet Battle Manual* (1977) and *Star Fleet Battles* (Task Force Games, 1979; later Amarillo Design Bureau) were licensed from FJ / Franz Joseph Designs. Steve Cole says Task Force more or less stumbled onto FJD's phone number [S20]. Wikipedia cites Appelcline's *Designers & Dragons* (p.114) and says FJ was uneasy about the game's violence [S10]. ADB later reached a separate agreement with Paramount [S20].
- **Ideas later adopted on screen.** Karen's list: an auxiliary or battle bridge, multiple and cargo transporters, a bridge "head" and an escape ladder, the boomerang badge used fleet-wide, and other classes of saucer-and-nacelle ships [S3 p.5]. **SINGLE-SOURCE**, and these are her interpretation.
- **Wider use.** The Manual was reportedly used or reviewed by NASA, Grumman, the US Navy (bridge layout) and universities [S5], [S6], [S10]. **SINGLE-SOURCE** (FJ, anecdotal).

### Franz Joseph Designs after 1994; Karen Dick's stewardship

- **Who runs it (SINGLE-SOURCE, Karen).** Karen Dick, administrator of FJ's estate, ran Franz Joseph Designs. In 1999 it had licences with Ballantine, Amarillo Design Bureau and GameScience [S3 p.1].
- **Enforcement (SINGLE-SOURCE, Karen).** She sent cease-and-desist letters over unlicensed mugs, shirts, patches and photocopies of the Manual. A pin company dropped its UFP-logo pin rather than re-credit it. She said single, non-commercial fan uses were fine [S3 p.5].
- **Attempts to revive the work (SINGLE-SOURCE).** A planned CD-ROM "interactive Tech Manual" (1995) was blocked because Paramount would not license the TOS elements [S3 p.5]. She also tried to add a foreword and pushed Ballantine to reprint [S3 p.6].
- **Other roles.** She was founding chair of Costume-Con and co-ran the CASTLE BLOOD haunted attraction [S3 p.1]. In 2004 she gave the Pax Team 21 interview on *Planet Earth* props, signed "Franz Joseph Designs" [S9].
- **Current status: not found.** No source after the early 2000s confirms whether FJD's licences continue today. ADB's materials still refer to an FJD licence (via search summaries) [S20].

## 5. Interviews with Franz Joseph or his family

1. **Gerry Williams and Penny Durrans, "These Will Be a Reality Sooner Than You Think."**
   - Conducted 15 February 1976. Published in *Subspace Chatter* #8 (Interstellar Trading Post, San Diego), October 1976. Fanzine.
   - URL: http://www.trekplace.com/fj-fjwilliamsint01.html
   - FJ's earliest known account of the origin story (S.T.A.R., "show us," the Blueprints as an afterthought) and his theory of warp drive. He says the Manual had been #1 since mid-December. He disowns fandom but wants to keep the fiction alive for young readers, and says he would stop if the Manual competed with Roddenberry's *Star Trek*.

2. **Paul Newitt, "An Interview with Franz Joseph."**
   - Face-to-face and tape-recorded. Newitt's introduction is dated October 1982. Published in James Van Hise's *Enterprise Incidents*, "Spotlight on the Technical Side" special. **Date disputed:** trekplace says June 1984; the Pax Team 21 site says issue #12, 1983.
   - URLs: http://www.trekplace.com/fj-fjnewittint01.html and http://www.trekplace.com/fj-fjnewittint02.html
   - The most detailed first-person account: the 14 April 1973 meeting, T.O.s sent to Roddenberry, *Planet Earth*, Mindling's film offer, and the contract ban on the words "Star Trek" (the over-jacket). Also the red colour chip, his hours, and why he used the pen name. He answers the "military" and "credit line" grievances and says he heard of them only second-hand.

3. **Greg Tyler, interview with Karen Dick (six pages).**
   - Conducted by email, June–July 1999. Published on trekplace.com, 1999, with updates in 2000, 2001 and 2002.
   - URL: http://www.trekplace.com/fj-kdint01.html (pages 01–06)
   - The fullest biography: christened name, Convair career, layoff, death. It also gives the family's detailed account of the Roddenberry/Paramount rift, the canon dispute, the "fan kook" episode and Okuda's later reconciliation, and Karen's management of Franz Joseph Designs.

4. **Karen Dick and Greg Tyler, "Franz Joseph Timeline."**
   - 1999. A chronology condensed from FJ's "Writing Activities Log" (1973–88) and his *Planet Earth* notebook, with notes by Tyler.
   - URL: http://www.trekplace.com/fj-timeline.html
   - Not an interview, but the closest thing to a primary document. It gives day-by-day dates for the Roddenberry letters, Equicon '74 sales, the Ballantine and Paramount contracts, print runs and the NYT #1. It is the source of most of the numbers in this report.

5. **Pax Team 21 (Brent Fox), "The Props of Planet Earth – Interview with Karen Dick."**
   - Conducted by email, September 2004. Fan website.
   - URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20090226020143/http://paxteam21.home.att.net/PE/invkd.html
   - Covers FJ's 1974 prop designs for Roddenberry's *Planet Earth* (Medipak, Multiceiver, Transceiver), 31 pages of drawings. Roddenberry asked for them to be back-dated and labelled as *Star Trek* pre-production work. The studio's rushed props fell far short of the drawings.

## 6. Unresolved or conflicting claims

1. **Birth name.** Francis Joseph (Karen) or Franz Anton Joseph (Wikipedia/NUMIDENT), and "Josef" on Memory Alpha.
2. **Nationality.** "German technical artist" (Memory Alpha) against Chicago-born (Karen; FamilySearch records cited by Wikipedia). The evidence favours Chicago.
3. **Length and title of his Convair job.** About 30 years as a design engineer (FJ, Karen) against "twenty plus years" as a technical artist (Newitt's introduction). The 1941–1969 dates give about 28 years.
4. **First letter to Roddenberry.** 14 May 1973 (timeline) against "June 3rd" (FJ 1982) against 22 June 1973 (FJ's notes on the Articles and Dreadnought mailing). These may be separate mailings.
5. **Where his reference material came from.** Wikipedia's "Majel Barrett gave access to original props and studio blueprints" has no support in the family or FJ sources, which describe slides, film clips, Whitfield and the Lincoln drawing set.
6. **The Blueprints' date.** Drawn 1973, private edition April 1974, Ballantine April (Memory Alpha) or May (timeline, Rehak) 1975. They were not published in 1973.
7. **Manual release date.** 1 November 1975 (Memory Alpha, the nominal publication date) against 26 November 1975 in stores (timeline).
8. **The NYT #1 claim.** Every source traces back to FJ and his family. Which list ("trade paperback" in Wikipedia) and how long (about four weeks to two to three months) are unverified against the NYT archive. Wikipedia's profitability-record and caused-the-movie claims are flagged there as original research, and the film contract (March 1975) came before the book.
9. **Print-run figures.** 450,000 first printing (timeline, via Rehak). The *Publishers Weekly* figures come second-hand through the timeline. FJ said about 40,000 first-printing copies were still unsold around 1982.
10. **Binder versus paperback.** "Vinyl-jacketed hardcover" (Memory Alpha), "paperback in a rigid plastic binder" (Wikipedia) and "paperback" (Goodreads). Booksellers usually list it as hardcover. These are differences in terminology about the same object.
11. **ISBN 0-345-27050-9.** Memory Alpha calls it "softback"; Goodreads calls it a 185-page hardcover. One Biblio listing gives a "1976 paperback" under the original ISBN. The pre-1986 printing history is unclear.
12. **Page count.** 192, 194 or 185. The book has no printed folios.
13. **The Mindling film offer.** 13 December 1976 (timeline) against January 1977 (FJ's recollection).
14. **Newitt interview publication.** *Enterprise Incidents* June 1984 special (trekplace) against issue #12, 1983 (Pax Team 21).
15. **Size of the master outline.** 50,000 subjects (1976) against 150,000 T.O.s (1982).
16. **Dreadnought in *Star Trek II*.** Not supported. At most its text and data block appear on a *Star Trek III* display (Tyler), and Ex Astris says it was never rendered. The 61 Cygni plaque in *II* is single-source (Newitt intro). No source supports member-planet flags as *II* set dressing.
17. **Star Trek Welcommittee.** No connection found. The relevant group is S.T.A.R. (Star Trek Association for Revival). Its national founding is variously dated (Detroit, May 1972 per Fancyclopedia, via search summary). The San Diego chapter dates to 1972.
18. **Roddenberry's credit complaint.** The timeline records a letter from Roddenberry (20 August 1975) about the credit. FJ said in 1982 that Roddenberry never raised it with him personally.
